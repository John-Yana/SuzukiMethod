About the studio

Music first, then notation.

The Suzuki approach rests on one simple belief: every child learns their mother tongue effortlessly — by listening, copying and being encouraged with love. Music can be learned the very same way. Our lessons grow that natural ability into real musicianship.

The philosophy

“Where love is deep, much can be accomplished.”

Founded by violinist Shinichi Suzuki in mid-20th-century Japan, the method treats the parent, child and teacher as one team. The parent is the home teacher; the ear leads before the eye; and skills are built through joyful review.

Core principles

Five ideas that make it work

Each lesson gently weaves these together, turning practice into a habit a child looks forward to.

01

Listen first

Students hear recordings of their pieces again and again before touching the keys, absorbing pitch and rhythm the way they absorb a favourite song.

02

The triangle partnership

Parent, child and teacher form one team. A parent sits in on each lesson, takes notes and coaches practice at home.

03

Listen before you read

Sheet-music reading is delayed until a strong ear, good posture and natural technique are firmly established.

04

Continuous review

Older pieces are revisited while new ones are added, deepening memory, tonal beauty and expressive phrasing.

05

Private & group

Private lessons refine personal technique; regular group classes build ensemble listening, confidence and community.

For parents

“What do I actually have to do?”

You don't need to be a musician — you're the home coach. Here's how it works.

Do I need to know music?

No. You just learn alongside, attend lessons, and gently guide practice at home. Many parents pick up more music than they ever expected.

How much practice is needed?

A little and often beats a lot and rarely. Short daily sessions that end on a positive note keep children keen — your teacher will tailor the rhythm.

What instrument do we need?

For beginners, a good acoustic or digital piano at home is ideal. We'll happily advise on options for your space and budget.

What ages do you teach?

We teach children from three years old up. The earliest years are the sweet spot for ear-first learning — but a child of any age can begin and thrive.
